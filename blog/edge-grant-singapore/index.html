EDGE Grant Singapore: what replaces EDG, PSG and MRA from 30 September 2026

Last updated: 23 September 2026

BizGrants Consulting · 23 September 2026 · 9 min read

The short answer: from 30 September 2026, Enterprise Singapore’s EDGE Grant replaces the Enterprise Development Grant (EDG), the Productivity Solutions Grant (PSG) and the Market Readiness Assistance (MRA) grant. EDG, MRA and PSG cease on 29 September 2026. Instead of working out which of the three grants a project belongs to, businesses apply for the activity they plan to carry out, across eight business areas, with up to S$100,000 in total grant support per year. Several details, including the support rate for each activity, have not yet been published.

This guide sets out what Enterprise Singapore has confirmed, what is still open, and what EDGE does not cover. People costs are the main gap: hiring, reskilling and job redesign sit with separate SkillsFuture schemes run by the Skills and Workforce Development Agency (SWDA).

EDGE Grant at a glance

What Enterprise Singapore has confirmed
Replaces Enterprise Development Grant (EDG), Productivity Solutions Grant (PSG) and Market Readiness Assistance (MRA)
Old schemes cease 29 September 2026
Apply under EDGE from 30 September 2026
Who can apply All Singapore businesses, including non-SMEs. The applicant must be a business entity registered in Singapore
Annual cap Up to S$100,000 in total grant support per year, shared across all activities and refreshed on 1 April
Support levels Differ by activity. No single EDGE-wide rate has been published
Scope Eight business areas and over 100 supportable activities
Payment Reimbursement, claimed once the activity is completed and fully paid for

What changes on 29 and 30 September 2026

Enterprise Singapore’s wording is short: “EDG, MRA and PSG will cease on 29 September 2026. From 30 September onwards, apply for business grant support under the EDGE Grant.” Until then, applications for the three existing grants continue through the Business Grants Portal.

If you already have something in the system, it is protected. Enterprise Singapore states that ongoing EDG, MRA and PSG submissions and projects will continue to be processed, and that claims can be submitted when the project is complete. What has not been published is a cut-off time on 29 September, or what happens to an application saved as a draft but not submitted. If you hold a quotation you intend to use under PSG, EDG or MRA, submit before 29 September rather than on the day.

The change was announced as part of the Budget 2026 package. Enterprise Singapore’s factsheet on the Business Refresh Package described EDGE as a single grant scheme that lets businesses “apply for funding based on their intended activities, such as enhancing digitalisation capabilities, expanding into new markets, or improving enterprise efficiencies,” instead of first deciding which grant their activities fall under.

What the EDGE Grant funds

Enterprise Singapore describes EDGE as a grant for Singapore companies starting new projects to upgrade capabilities, improve efficiency and expand overseas. It is organised into eight business areas. The EDGE page names seven of them:

  • Automation & Digitalisation
  • Business Strategy
  • Financial Management
  • Innovation
  • Internationalisation
  • Standards
  • Sustainability

The activity count differs between official sources. The EDGE page refers to over 100 activities, while a Ministry of Trade and Industry speech put it at over 150 supportable activities. The full activity list, with the terms for each, is published by Enterprise Singapore rather than summarised here.

Two changes from the old schemes are already clear:

  • Overseas expansion no longer has to target a new market. Under EDGE, the “new market” criterion for activities previously supported under MRA is removed, so enterprises can also deepen their presence in overseas markets they already serve.
  • Pre-approved digital solutions are carrying over in a new format. IMDA has required every PSG pre-approved vendor to move its solutions to a flexible package structure in preparation for EDGE. Solutions that were not migrated are delisted.

How much the EDGE Grant funds

The confirmed figure is the cap: up to S$100,000 in total grant support per year, across all activities. The cap is shared across everything a company applies for and is refreshed on 1 April each year. Grant support is paid by reimbursement, and a claim can be submitted once the activity is completed and full payment has been made.

The support rate is less clear than many summaries suggest. Enterprise Singapore’s EDGE page says support levels differ by activity. The rates of up to 70 per cent for SMEs and up to 50 per cent for non-SMEs that appeared in Budget 2026 were announced for schemes that help businesses go overseas, such as MRA, the Business Adaptation Grant and the Global Innovation Alliance. They were not stated as a rate for every EDGE activity. Until Enterprise Singapore publishes the rate for each activity, treat any single headline percentage for EDGE with caution.

Companies that need more support than the standard amount, for example for customised projects, can submit their applications to Enterprise Singapore, which assesses them case by case.

Who can apply

EDGE is available to all Singapore businesses, including non-SMEs. That is a real change: the PSG and MRA were built around SMEs. The one company criterion Enterprise Singapore has published for EDGE is that the applicant must be a business entity registered in Singapore. It adds that other requirements may apply depending on the supportable activity, and that more details would be provided at launch.

The old grants used tests that may or may not carry over. EDG required at least 30 per cent local equity held by Singapore Citizens or Permanent Residents. PSG and MRA defined an SME by that local equity test plus group annual sales of up to S$100 million or group employment of up to 200. Enterprise Singapore has not yet confirmed which definition EDGE uses to set the SME rate, so check the terms of the specific activity before you rely on it.

How to apply

The application route on launch day has not been confirmed in the material published so far. Two things are known. First, the Business Grants Portal, at apply.gov.sg/grants/business, handles EDG, MRA and PSG applications until they cease. Second, the Ministry of Trade and Industry announced that the new BizSG portal goes live on 30 September, with a grant recommender that points a business to relevant grants and activities from Enterprise Singapore and SWDA based on its needs and goals.

One rule is worth assuming until EDGE’s own terms say otherwise. Under the predecessor grants, applications were not supported if the project had started, a payment or deposit had been made, or a contract had been signed before the application was submitted. Collect quotations, but do not sign or pay until you have applied.

What the EDGE Grant does not cover: people

None of the named EDGE business areas is about hiring, reskilling or redesigning jobs, and no official EDGE material says it funds them. That support sits in separate schemes run by SWDA, with their own caps:

  • Job redesign. Job redesign support used to sit inside the PSG, as PSG-JR, which closed on 31 December 2025. It was replaced by the SkillsFuture Workforce Development Grant (Job Redesign+), which funds up to 70 per cent for SMEs and 50 per cent for non-SMEs, capped at S$150,000 per enterprise. So job redesign does not carry into EDGE through the PSG.
  • Salary support for new or reskilled roles. The Career Conversion Programme funds salary support while a new hire or an existing employee is trained into a new or redesigned role, with higher support for workers aged 40 and above or long-term unemployed.
  • The SkillsFuture Enterprise Credit. The current credit lists EDG, MRA and PSG among the programmes it offsets. Final claims under the current credit are due by 30 November 2026, and from 1 December 2026 a redesigned credit run by SWDA gives eligible employers a fresh S$10,000 for workforce transformation. Whether either version can offset your share of an EDGE project has not been stated. Our SFEC expiry guide covers the deadline.

In practice, a transformation project often needs both halves. EDGE can fund the system, the overseas push or the certification, while the workforce grants fund the redesign of roles and the salaries of people being trained into them. Plan the two tracks together, and keep each cost item under one grant only. Our workforce transformation page sets out the people side.

What to do this week

  • Submitting under PSG, EDG or MRA? Submit before 29 September rather than on the day. Ongoing submissions continue to be processed.
  • Mid-way through an approved project? Carry on. Enterprise Singapore says ongoing projects continue and claims are made on completion.
  • Planning something new? Get quotations, but do not sign, pay a deposit or start work before applying under EDGE.
  • Holding current SkillsFuture Enterprise Credit? Note the 30 November 2026 final-claims date.
  • Redesigning roles or hiring into them? Look at WDG(JR+) and the Career Conversion Programme, not EDGE.

What is still not confirmed

As at 23 September 2026, the following have not been set out in the official material we could find. Check them against Enterprise Singapore’s EDGE page before committing spend:

  • The support rate for each activity, and whether any rate applies across EDGE as a whole
  • The SME definition EDGE uses to set the SME rate
  • The name of the eighth business area
  • Whether EDGE applications are submitted through the Business Grants Portal, the BizSG portal or both
  • Processing times for applications and claims
  • Whether the S$100,000 cap applies per company or per group, and how grants approved earlier in the year count towards it
  • What happens to applications saved as drafts but not submitted by 29 September

IMDA’s vendor guidance also refers to EDGE launching in October 2026, while Enterprise Singapore states that businesses apply under EDGE from 30 September 2026. Take Enterprise Singapore’s date as the one that governs applications.

Frequently asked questions

  • Q: What is the EDGE Grant?
    A: The EDGE Grant is Enterprise Singapore’s single business grant. It streamlines the Market Readiness Assistance, Productivity Solutions Grant and Enterprise Development Grant into one scheme. Businesses apply by the activity they plan, such as digitalising or expanding overseas, across eight business areas, with up to S$100,000 in total support each year.
  • Q: When do EDG, PSG and MRA end?
    A: Enterprise Singapore states that EDG, MRA and PSG cease on 29 September 2026, and that from 30 September 2026 businesses apply for business grant support under the EDGE Grant. No cut-off time has been published, so submit any application under the old schemes before 29 September rather than on the day.
  • Q: What happens to my ongoing PSG, EDG or MRA application?
    A: It continues. Enterprise Singapore says ongoing EDG, MRA and PSG submissions and projects will continue to be processed, and claims can be submitted when the project is complete. Nothing has been published on unsubmitted drafts or quotations, so treat anything not submitted in time as a new EDGE application.
  • Q: How much does the EDGE Grant fund?
    A: Up to S$100,000 in total grant support per year, shared across all activities and refreshed on 1 April each year. Support levels differ by activity. The 70 per cent SME and 50 per cent non-SME rates announced in Budget 2026 were stated for internationalisation schemes, and no single EDGE-wide rate has been published.
  • Q: Can non-SMEs apply for the EDGE Grant?
    A: Yes. Enterprise Singapore states that EDGE is available to all Singapore businesses, including non-SMEs. The applicant must be a business entity registered in Singapore, and other requirements may apply depending on the supportable activity. Enterprise Singapore said more details would be provided at launch.
  • Q: Does the EDGE Grant fund hiring, training or job redesign?
    A: No official EDGE material says it does, and none of the named EDGE business areas covers people. Job redesign support moved from the PSG to the SkillsFuture Workforce Development Grant (Job Redesign+), and salary support for new or reskilled roles sits with the Career Conversion Programme, both run by SWDA.
  • Q: Can I sign a contract before my EDGE application is approved?
    A: Avoid it. The predecessor grants did not support applications where a contract was signed, a payment or deposit was made, or the project had started before submission. EDGE-specific rules on this are not yet confirmed, so obtain quotations but do not commit until you have applied.

Where to check current terms

Figures here reflect official material published as at 23 September 2026. Confirm current terms with Enterprise Singapore’s EDGE Grant page, Enterprise Singapore on Budget 2026, the Business Refresh Package factsheet, and the Ministry of Trade and Industry. For the workforce side, see WDG(JR+).
