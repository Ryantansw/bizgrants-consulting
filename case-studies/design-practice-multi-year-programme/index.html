A multi-year conversion programme at a design practice, including a re-route after a decline

Case / 2025−19 Architecture / interior design practice Multi-year programme · channel re-route

A design practice used Career Conversion Programme support repeatedly across several years and multiple candidates, rather than as a one-off response to a single hire. Over that period the practice converted staff into sustainability, project delivery, and studio leadership scopes.

Industry context

Design practices carry a specific structural problem: senior staff accumulate scope organically. A project architect quietly becomes the person who handles sustainability certification, or client development, or delivery oversight, and nothing in the employment record reflects it. That drift is both a retention risk and, handled properly, a legitimate job redesign case.

What changed and why CCP applied

Several candidates had scope that had genuinely moved beyond their contracted role, particularly toward sustainability and environmental design, an area where the practice was building capability in response to client and regulatory demand. Others were new hires stepping into scopes the practice had not previously staffed.

Our strategy

Because the practice was returning to the programme repeatedly, the emphasis was on consistency: each application had to be defensible on its own terms, but also had to sit coherently alongside the ones before it. A firm that describes the same role differently across two submissions invites questions it does not want. One application was declined under its first channel. Rather than treat that as the end, the role was reassessed against a different pathway with different eligibility criteria and resubmitted. The reassessment was substantive, because the role genuinely fitted the second channel better, not a resubmission of the same argument to a different reader.

OJT activities included

  • Sustainability and environmental design scopes formalised into contracted job descriptions.
  • Promotion letters and contract variations used as evidence of genuine, documented scope change.
  • One declined application reassessed against an alternative pathway and resubmitted.
  • Applications sequenced across years so each built on a consistent company-level position.

Outcome

Roles
Sustainability, project delivery, studio leadership and support scopes across multiple candidates
Duration
Programme sustained across several years
Application status
Multiple approvals; one declined application re-routed to an alternative channel
Strategic value
Conversion support embedded into ordinary workforce planning rather than used reactively

Key takeaway

A decline is usually a routing problem, not a verdict on the role. The question worth asking is not how to argue it better but whether it was ever the right channel, and often it was not.
