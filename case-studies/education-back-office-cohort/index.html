Professionalising the back office of an education group

Case / 2026−23 Private education / enrichment Five-candidate cohort · back-office professionalisation

An education group had built a strong teaching operation and, alongside it, an administrative function assembled from whoever was available. Five roles across HR, finance, and operations were converted into properly scoped positions with structured training behind them.

Industry context

Education businesses scale on teaching quality, and their support functions typically lag by years. The pattern is familiar: an administrator gradually absorbs payroll, then HR compliance, then finance reconciliation, without ever being trained in any of them. It works until it does not, usually at an audit, a dispute, or a growth step.

What changed and why CCP applied

Back-office roles are sometimes assumed to be outside the scope of conversion funding. They are not, provided the redesign is genuine. In this case each role was being pulled apart and rebuilt: HR operations separated from general administration, finance and accounting scoped as a discipline rather than a task, and each position given a defined competency set for the first time.

Our strategy

The group’s own people-capability position was assessed first, which established both the baseline the training would improve on and the credibility of the redesign. Roles were then sequenced rather than submitted together, with the clearest case first, so the pattern of documentation was established and tested before the more marginal roles followed. Because several candidates were internal staff whose scope had drifted, the evidence emphasis was on contracts, appointment letters, and contribution history showing the trajectory, rather than on the new job description alone.

OJT activities included

  • Organisation-level people-capability self-assessment completed ahead of role scoping.
  • Five role-specific training plans across HR operations, finance, accounting, and administration.
  • Existing contracts and appointment letters used to evidence genuine scope trajectory for internal candidates.
  • Submissions sequenced rather than batched.

Outcome

Roles
HR operations, finance, accounting, and administrative scopes across five candidates
Approach
Sequenced submissions on a common organisational position
Application status
Cohort progressed with role-specific training plans
Strategic value
Support functions rebuilt as disciplines rather than accumulated duties

Key takeaway

Back-office roles are fundable. What disqualifies them is not the function. It is job descriptions that describe duties rather than competencies, which is how most administrative roles are written.
