A lawyer hired to build a law firm’s AI practice

Case / 2026−36 Legal services / legal AI Single role · AI competencies at the core

A small cross-border law practice was formalising AI and automation services for its clients. It hired a lawyer who had been working freelance and gave them a new remit: build generative AI applications, automate legal workflows, and advise clients on AI governance. The application was approved eight days after submission.

Industry context

Law firms are under pressure from both directions on AI. Clients want to know how to use it safely, and the firm’s own work is changing as drafting, review and research become partly automated. Few firms can hire a technologist who also understands legal practice, so the realistic route is to take a lawyer and build the technical capability on top.

What changed and why CCP applied

The previous work was legal advisory only. The new role keeps legal judgement as its foundation but adds work the candidate had never done: building generative AI applications, automating document and matter workflows, scoping AI projects with clients, and advising on governance. The assessment turned on those AI competencies, which were substantial and entirely new to the candidate. That is what separates a conversion from a lawyer simply using new tools.

Our strategy

We wrote the job description so that the AI work was the core of the role rather than an add-on to legal practice, and attached an annex mapping the AI skills to recognised competency standards. Because the candidate had freelanced before joining, their full employment history was documented up front, with a signed declaration covering the freelance period. The on-the-job plan was built around live client work, so each AI competency had a real deliverable attached to it.

OJT activities included

  • Building generative AI applications for legal tasks and testing them on real matters.
  • Automating document and matter workflows inside the practice.
  • Scoping AI adoption projects with clients, including governance and risk.
  • Presenting AI options and their limits to non-technical clients.

Outcome

Role
Legal consultant converted into an associate lawyer and legal AI practitioner
Pathway
Different job scope pathway (new hire)
Status
Approved eight days after submission
Strategic value
An AI service line led by someone who understands both the law and the tools

Key takeaway

When AI is the reason a role exists, put the AI work at the centre of the job description and map it to recognised competencies. A lawyer who uses AI tools is not a conversion. A lawyer whose job is now to build and govern them is.
