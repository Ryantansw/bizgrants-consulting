From the surgical clinic floor to business development

Case / 2025−41 Healthcare / specialist clinic Single role · clinical to commercial

A private oncology clinic group hired a surgical clinic nurse into a business development role. The move from clinical care to building referral networks and running campaigns is about as clear a change of function as conversion support sees. The application was approved and taken through to the claim.

Industry context

Specialist clinics depend on referrals from general practitioners, other specialists and partner organisations. Growing them needs someone who can explain the clinic’s services credibly to clinicians, which is why clinical staff often make strong business development hires, and why they need structured support to learn the commercial side.

What changed and why CCP applied

Clinical nursing and clinic operations were removed from the role. The new scope covered referral partner development, digital campaigns and growth proposals. The candidate’s clinical knowledge stayed useful, but none of the day-to-day work carried over.

Our strategy

We prepared the on-the-job plan and application documents around the referral-building work the clinic needed. Administrative queries during review, including an outdated human capital diagnostic and a declaration that had to be re-signed, were resolved before they could hold up approval. At claim stage the claim was timed so that the final month’s payroll records could be included.

OJT activities included

  • Market and competitor study of the clinic’s referral landscape.
  • Meetings with referral partners, followed by quarterly partner updates.
  • Campaign planning across digital channels.
  • Designing and running one growth initiative end to end.

Outcome

Role
Surgical clinic nurse converted into business development executive
Pathway
Different job scope pathway (new hire)
Status
Approved; claim submitted and confirmed in order by the programme partner
Strategic value
A clinician-led referral development function

Key takeaway

Clinical-to-commercial moves are strong conversion cases because almost nothing in the daily work carries over. The risk is administrative, not substantive, so the paperwork deserves the same care as the training plan.
